A Continuous Glucose Monitor (such as Dexcom G7 or FreeStyle Libre 3) uses a water-resistant sensor placed on the upper arm or abdomen. A flexible micro-filament measures glucose in the interstitial fluid between your cells every 60 seconds and transmits readings to your smartphone.

No routine fingersticks are needed! Modern CGMs are factory calibrated. You only need a traditional fingerstick if:

  • Your symptoms do not match your sensor reading (e.g., feeling dizzy/shaky when sensor reads 110 mg/dL).
  • During the first 2 hours of sensor warm-up.
  • If your sensor displays a "Brief Sensor Issue" or error code.

An Automated Insulin Delivery (AID) system—such as Tandem Control-IQ, Omnipod 5, or Medtronic MiniMed 780G—combines an insulin pump with a continuous glucose sensor via a smart mathematical algorithm.

Every 5 minutes, the algorithm predicts where your blood glucose will be 30 minutes in the future. It automatically:

  • Increases or delivers micro-boluses when blood sugar is trending high.
  • Reduces or halts insulin delivery when blood sugar drops toward hypoglycemia.

Clinical studies show AID systems achieve over 70% Time-in-Range (70-180 mg/dL) and substantially eliminate overnight low blood sugars.

Modern incretin mimetics (such as once-weekly semaglutide and dual GLP-1/GIP tirzepatide) work on natural hormonal pathways originating in the digestive tract and brain:

  • Glucose-Dependent Insulin Release: Promotes insulin secretion only when blood glucose is elevated, presenting minimal hypoglycemia risk.
  • Glucagon Suppression: Halts abnormal hepatic glucose dumping from the liver.
  • Satiety Signaling: Interacts with appetite centers in the hypothalamus to reduce cravings and "food noise".
  • Cardio-Renal Protection: Proven to reduce cardiovascular event risk and preserve chronic kidney filtration.

When blood glucose drops below 70 mg/dL, treat immediately following the Rule of 15:

  1. Consume 15 grams of fast-acting simple carbohydrates (e.g., 4 oz fruit juice, 4 oz non-diet soda, or 3-4 glucose tablets). Avoid chocolate or peanut butter as fats delay sugar absorption.
  2. Wait 15 minutes resting quietly.
  3. Re-check your blood glucose with a fingerstick.
  4. If still below 70 mg/dL, repeat with another 15g of carbohydrates.
Emergency Warning: For severe hypoglycemia with altered mental status or unconsciousness, administer emergency nasal glucagon (Baqsimi) or auto-injector glucagon immediately and call 911.

Both conditions involve hormonal imbalances from the butterfly-shaped thyroid gland:

Hypothyroidism (Underactive)

High TSH, Low Free T4. Symptoms include chronic fatigue, unexpected weight gain, cold intolerance, dry skin, constipation, and brain fog (most commonly Hashimoto’s thyroiditis).

Hyperthyroidism (Overactive)

Suppressed TSH, High Free T3/T4. Symptoms include racing heart rate, palpitations, tremor, unintentional weight loss, heat sensitivity, and sleep disruption (most commonly Graves’ disease).

Over 90% of thyroid nodules are completely benign (non-cancerous). We use the American College of Radiology (ACR) TI-RADS ultrasound grading system to determine when a biopsy is clinically justified:

  • Nodules ≥1.0 cm with highly suspicious ultrasound features (marked hypoechogenicity, punctate microcalcifications, irregular margins, or taller-than-wide shape).
  • Nodules ≥1.5 cm with moderately suspicious features.
  • Nodules ≥2.5 cm with mildly suspicious features.

Our in-office FNA takes just 15 minutes under gentle local anesthesia with zero downtime.

Standard commercial laboratory TSH reference ranges are remarkably wide (typically 0.45 to 4.50 mIU/L). However, research demonstrates that individuals often feel optimal within a narrower personalized range (often 1.0 to 2.0 mIU/L).

Furthermore, our endocrinologists evaluate the complete thyroid axis:

  • Free T3 (Active Hormone): Assessing cellular conversion of T4 into active T3.
  • Thyroid Antibodies (Anti-TPO, Anti-TG): Quantifying autoimmune Hashimoto's inflammation.
  • Metabolic Micronutrients: Checking ferritin (iron storage), Vitamin D, and B12, which directly impact thyroid receptor binding.

PCOS is diagnosed using the standardized Rotterdam Criteria (requiring at least two of: irregular ovulatory cycles, clinical or biochemical androgen excess, and polycystic ovarian morphology).

Because PCOS is deeply intertwined with hyperinsulinemia and insulin resistance, our medical management targets root metabolic causes:

  • Insulin-sensitizing pharmacotherapy (Metformin, inositols).
  • Anti-androgen therapy (spironolactone) for hirsutism and androgenic alopecia.
  • Cycle regulation and pre-conception ovulatory co-management with fertility specialists.

Fasting requirements depend entirely on the specific laboratory biomarkers ordered for your visit:

Fasting Required (8-12 Hours): Fasting blood glucose, comprehensive lipid profiles (triglycerides/cholesterol), fasting insulin, and dynamic endocrine stimulation tests. Water is strongly encouraged!
No Fasting Needed: Point-of-care HbA1c tests, routine thyroid panels (TSH, Free T3/T4), and DEXA bone density scans do NOT require fasting.

Please follow these specific rules for morning medications:

  • Blood Pressure & Heart Medications: Take them at your normal scheduled time with plain water.
  • Thyroid Medications (Levothyroxine / Synthroid / Liothyronine): HOLD your morning dose until AFTER your blood draw, as circulating T4/T3 spikes temporarily 2–4 hours after ingestion.
  • Diabetes Medications & Insulin: If fasting for lab work, hold meal-time rapid insulin and oral sulfonylureas until after you eat. Take your normal basal (long-acting) insulin unless instructed otherwise.

Biotin (frequently found in hair, skin, and nail supplements, B-complexes, and multivitamins) causes significant analytical assay interference in laboratory immunoassay machines.

Excess circulating biotin artificially causes:

  • Falsely suppressed TSH levels (mimicking hyperthyroidism).
  • Falsely elevated Free T3 and Free T4 readings.
  • Skewed cortisol, testosterone, and cardiac troponin lab results.
Instruction: Please discontinue all biotin supplements at least 72 hours (3 full days) prior to any scheduled endocrine blood draw.

A Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry (DEXA) scan is the gold-standard test for diagnosing osteopenia and osteoporosis. You remain comfortably clothed while lying on a cushioned table as an imaging arm passes over your lower spine and hips.

Radiation is negligible: A single DEXA scan emits less than 1 to 5 microsieverts of radiation—less than 1/10th of a single standard chest X-ray and less than the natural atmospheric background radiation you receive during an airline flight.

The examination requires under 15 minutes with immediate T-score and Trabecular Bone Score (TBS) microarchitecture reporting.

Visit Preparation & Fasting Protocols

Accurate endocrine diagnosis depends upon pristine laboratory conditions. Follow these 4 evidence-based preparation rules before stepping into our clinic.

8 - 12 Hours Fasting

Metabolic & Lipid Panels

For fasting blood glucose, fasting insulin, and lipid panels, fast overnight. Do NOT drink juice, coffee, or milk. Water is encouraged to keep veins hydrated for smooth phlebotomy.

Note: Routine HbA1c testing does NOT require fasting.
Medication Timing

Thyroid Hormone Morning Dose

Hold your morning levothyroxine, Synthroid, or Armour Thyroid until after your blood draw. Ingesting hormone tablets causes temporary circulating spikes that distort Free T4 readings.

Stop Biotin supplements 72h prior to avoid assay interference.
24-Hour Calcium Hold

DEXA Bone Density Scan

Discontinue calcium supplements and calcium-rich antacids (Tums) for 24-48 hours prior to your DEXA scan. Undissolved calcium tablets in the stomach create density artifacts on spinal images.

Wear loose clothing without metal buttons or zippers.

Diabetes Tech & Sensor Troubleshooting

Encountering a sensor alert or pump error? Use our clinical triage steps to quickly diagnose issues and avoid unnecessary interruptions in care.

"Brief Sensor Issue" or Gaps

CGM Sensor

Temporary sensor signal dropouts typically stem from localized interstitial fluid displacement, dehydration, or "compression lows" caused by sleeping directly on your arm sensor.

Step 1: Drink 16 oz of water and wait up to 3 hours without removing the sensor.
Step 2: Check blood glucose via traditional fingerstick if symptomatic.
Step 3: If the error persists beyond 3 hours, replace the sensor and submit a free replacement ticket directly via the Dexcom or Abbott app.

Unexplained High BG on Pump

Pump Triage

Sudden unexpected blood glucose above 250 mg/dL that fails to respond to pump boluses indicates an infusion set failure, kinked cannula, or degraded insulin.

The 2-Hour Rule: If a pump correction bolus does not decrease blood glucose in 2 hours, suspect occlusion.
Action: Administer a correction dose via manual syringe or pen. Immediately change the pump infusion site and reservoir.
Check Ketones: Test blood or urine ketones if BG remains >250 mg/dL to prevent DKA.

Adhesive Rash & Detachment

Skin Health

Sweating, active lifestyles, and medical adhesive allergies can cause sensors to peel prematurely or trigger localized contact dermatitis.

Skin Prep: Clean area with alcohol and allow to dry completely. Apply a barrier wipe (Skin-Tac or Cavilon No-Sting).
Overpatches: Apply breathable hypoallergenic waterproof overlay patches (Simpatch, Lexcam) on day 1.
Removal: Use adhesive release spray (TacAway or baby oil) to prevent skin tearing.

Airport Travel & TSA Scanners

Travel Safety

Continuous glucose monitors and insulin pump electronics are sensitive to high-intensity airport baggage X-ray radiation.

TSA Rule: Do NOT pass insulin pumps or CGM transmitters through baggage X-ray belts or millimeter-wave body scanners.
Hand Pat-Down: Inform TSA officers you are wearing medical devices and request a physical hand inspection.
Carry-On Only: Keep all insulin vials, pens, and glucagon in your carry-on bag—never in checked luggage where temperatures freeze.
